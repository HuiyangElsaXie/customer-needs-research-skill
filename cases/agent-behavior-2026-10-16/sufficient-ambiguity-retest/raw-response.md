# Customer needs brief — synthetic desk review

## Decision and scope
Investigate whether solo tutors need a clearer way to identify the accepted lesson time when changes are coordinated with parents. This is a landing-page research angle, not a validated positioning claim.

Product: proposed, untested shared scheduling (F0). Segment: solo tutors coordinating lesson changes with parents. Market: illustrative English-language market; geography unknown. Evidence window: supplied notes dated 2026-10-16; individual event dates unknown. The permitted source is brief.md, containing invented paraphrases rather than real interviews. No external research was performed. No further intake is necessary to choose a reversible research direction.

## What the evidence supports
**Selected need:** Know which lesson slot has been accepted after a change is discussed across messages.

- **Observed evidence:** F1/P01 reports several messages associated with shifting work hours, copying the final slot to a calendar, and sometimes asking again which slot was accepted. Whose work hours shift is unknown. F3/P03 reports two proposed slots in different threads and unclear final parent acceptance. These are labeled paraphrases, not quotations.
- **Consequence and workaround:** F1 includes repeat confirmation and calendar copying. F3 includes uncertainty about acceptance; no workaround or downstream harm is stated. Desired clarity is an interpretation of these notes, not a separately reported customer outcome.
- **Recurrence:** Two distinct fixtures, F1 and F3, support this theme among four participant notes. This is sample recurrence, not market prevalence, and synthetic recurrence cannot validate a real customer need.
- **Counterevidence:** F2/P02 has fixed weekly slots and no coordination difficulty; a calendar and occasional message suffice. F4/P04 finds a slot easily but has unclear late-change policy. Thus, scheduling difficulty is not universal and policy clarity is a separate problem. F3's satisfactory billing tool provides no basis for a billing replacement angle.
- **Confidence:** Sufficient to choose a narrow hypothesis for investigation within this exercise; no real-world confidence or buying intent can be inferred.
- **Interpretation / marketing implication:** Explore an angle around clarity of the accepted time during lesson changes. Do not yet promise fewer messages, automatic calendar updates, fewer missed lessons, or confirmed parent acceptance: no product capability or outcome is validated.
- **Untested hypothesis:** Tutors dealing with changing arrangements find acceptance uncertainty consequential enough to seek a different approach. A shared scheduling product might address it, but neither solution fit nor willingness to pay is established.

**Other supported candidate:** Late-change policy clarity appears in F4 only. Its consequence, workaround, desired resolution, and buyer relevance are unknown. Prioritize acceptance clarity because two notes describe it and F1 supplies a concrete extra action. This ranking reflects the supplied sample and decision relevance, not measured severity or market demand.

## Coverage and next step

Real tutors, parents, buyers, geographic variation, event frequency, severity, purchasing authority, and willingness to pay remain untested. Use these neutral questions with real tutors, anchored to a recent event:
1. Tell me about the most recent lesson-time change you coordinated with a parent.
2. How did you determine which time had been accepted?
3. What did you do after that, and what happened next?
4. Tell me about a recent change that went smoothly. What made it straightforward?
5. What, if anything, would make you change your current approach?
