# Customer needs brief

## Decision and scope
Investigate **knowing which changed lesson time the parent has finally accepted** as the single landing-page problem angle for LanternDesk, an untested proposed shared scheduling product for solo tutors coordinating with parents.

This is a desk-review draft based solely on supplied synthetic notes, not customer research or validation. The illustrative market is English-language; geography and buyer identity are unknown. The supplied evidence is dated 2026-10-16 and was accessed 2026-10-16; individual event dates are unknown. No public evidence window applies because no public research was permitted or performed. Product capabilities and outcomes remain unvalidated (F0).

## What the evidence supports
**Need and situation:** When lesson arrangements change and alternatives are discussed across messages, the tutor needs a clear record of the final accepted slot.

**Observed fixture evidence:** F1/P01 describes changing parent work hours, several messages, manual copying of the final slot into a calendar, and sometimes asking again which slot was accepted. F3/P03 describes two proposed slots in different threads and unclear final parent acceptance. These are labeled paraphrases from the supplied notes, not real interview quotations.

**Consequences and workarounds:** Extra clarification messages and uncertainty are explicit in F1; ambiguous acceptance is explicit in F3. Manual calendar copying is explicit only in F1. Missed lessons, lost revenue, time saved, and scheduling errors were not measured and must not be claimed.

**Desired outcome — interpretation:** Tutor and parent can identify the accepted time without reconstructing a conversation. This is inferred from the fixtures, not a stated or validated outcome.

**Recurrence and confidence:** Two distinct fixture participants, P01 and P03, support investigating this theme. Four participant notes were supplied; no duplicates are indicated. This is recurrence within a tiny synthetic sample, not market prevalence. Confidence is sufficient to choose a research question, and insufficient to establish real demand, severity, willingness to pay, or product effectiveness.

**Counterevidence and alternatives:** F2/P02 has fixed weekly slots and no coordination difficulty; calendar plus occasional messaging suffice. Exclude that situation from the initial research focus. F4/P04 finds slots easily but lacks a clear late-change policy: a separate policy need that shared scheduling may not solve. F3 reports satisfactory billing, so the evidence does not support a billing angle. Confirmation ambiguity has two supporting fixtures, versus one for policy uncertainty, and is directly relevant to scheduling. Actual comparative severity and frequency remain unknown.

**Marketing implication — proposed research direction:** Explore messaging about clarity of the accepted lesson time for tutors handling changes. A discussion prompt could be “Which lesson time is confirmed?” Do not publish promises that LanternDesk prevents confusion or saves time until capability and outcome evidence exist.

**Untested hypothesis:** Tutors with recent lesson changes experience enough confirmation uncertainty that a shared accepted-time record would improve their workflow. Existing calendar and messaging practices may already be adequate; test that alternative explicitly.

## Coverage and next step
Only brief.md was analyzed. No searches, interviews, external verification, account access, or inaccessible-source attempts occurred. The four distinct synthetic participant notes are illustrative and nonrepresentative. Parents, real tutors, buyers, geographic differences, actual event dates, recurrence rates, and willingness to pay are missing. There is no historical real-world evidence in this set.

Use these neutral questions in future authorized research:

1. Walk me through the last lesson-time change you coordinated with a parent, from the first message to the final arrangement.
2. How did each person know which time had been accepted, and where was that recorded?
3. What, if anything, required follow-up, and what happened as a result?
4. Tell me about a recent change that went smoothly. What helped?
5. How do you currently decide whether your scheduling tools are adequate, and who decides whether to pay for a different tool?

**One next validation step:** Conduct a small, separately authorized round of three recent-event interviews with real solo tutors who coordinated a lesson change in the past month. Ask the same questions, including smooth examples, and record anonymized evidence of ambiguity, workarounds, consequences, and adequate existing solutions. Do not contact anyone as part of this task. Continue with this angle only if concrete recent events reveal a consequential unmet need; otherwise revise or drop it. This round would refine the problem, not establish prevalence or validate LanternDesk.
