# Fictional example: customer needs

All businesses, participants, feedback, and dates below are invented to demonstrate structure. No research was conducted. Do not cite this as real evidence.

Decision: For fictional appointment tool LanternDesk, which problem should a draft landing page investigate for solo tutors?

Synthetic input F1, fictional interview note supplied for this demo, 2026-10-01: Participant P01 says rescheduling creates repeated messages with parents. This is a fictional paraphrase, not a real quote.
Synthetic input F2, same demo date: P02 reports no rescheduling difficulty because their calendar is stable.

Observation in this invented sample: one of two fictional participants reports message back-and-forth; the other is counterevidence.
Inference: tutors with frequently changing schedules may have a coordination need. Confidence is only illustrative and no real-world conclusion follows.
Hypothesis: a shared rescheduling flow could reduce coordination effort. The product capability and actual customer demand are unverified.
Marketing implication: investigate an angle about simpler schedule changes; do not publish a time-saving claim.
Next question: “Tell me about the last appointment you had to change. What happened?”
Limitations: synthetic inputs only, zero real participants, no market prevalence or willingness-to-pay evidence.
