## Marketing implication and next check

- **Observation inside the fixtures:** F1/F3 contain confirmation friction; F2 is a counterexample. There are zero real participant observations.
- **Inference:** tutors with changing schedules may need clearer agreement on the final time. This is conditional on synthetic inputs.
- **Hypothesis:** a shared flow might address that need. F0 provides no working-product proof or established benefit.
- **Draft research angle:** “How do you confirm a lesson's new time with a parent?” Use as a discovery question, not a claim that LanternDesk solves it.
- **Exclude:** “Saves tutors hours every week,” “most tutors struggle,” or “parents never miss a change.” None is supported.

Next proposed step, **not performed**: use neutral questions in a small permitted discovery round with tutors who have changing and fixed schedules. Reconsider N1 if current workarounds are consistently sufficient; reconsider the scope if policy clarity dominates.

1. Tell me about the last lesson you changed. What happened from the first request to the final agreement?
2. How did you know both sides had agreed the same time?
3. What worked well with your current method, and when was no extra follow-up needed?
4. What, if anything, was unclear about the change policy in that situation?
